Young-Yun Choi
Ph.D. Candidate, Department of Economics
Georgia State University

I am an applied microeconomist. My research examines what drives economic development and contributes to persistent inequality through three complementary strands: (i) the role of information and technology, (ii) the long-run effects of institutions and place-based policies, and (iii) economic measurement using satellite imagery, remote sensing, geospatial data, and artificial intelligence.

Together, these approaches make it possible to study questions and settings where conventional data are limited or unavailable. Each research strand has received external support: the Smith Richardson Foundation (SRF) for information and technology, the Economic History Association (EHA) for institutions, and the Korea Foundation for Advanced Studies (KFAS) Social Innovation Award for economic measurement.

Research Fields: Development Economics, Urban Economics, Economic History
